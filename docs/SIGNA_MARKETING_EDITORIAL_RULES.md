# SIGNA Marketing & Editorial Rules

Status: CURRENT
Owner / final approval: Cy
Applies to: SIGNA AI Lab website, 8-locale Article Clusters, product/landing copy, ASO-supporting website copy, distribution copy derived from website articles.

## 1. Primary objective

Every marketing article must help the intended reader understand why SIGNA is useful and make trying SIGNA feel like a natural next step.

Accuracy and claim-safety are mandatory, but they are guardrails, not the headline. Do not make limitations the main product message.

Default narrative:
1. Start with a real user situation or problem.
2. Explain why the problem matters in ordinary language.
3. Show the useful role of visible watermarking, the invisible SIGNA signal, Inspect, and/or on-device processing when relevant.
4. Make the benefit concrete: prepare before sharing; keep the image readable; add a signal without covering the image; inspect the signal/image state later; process supported images locally/on-device.
5. State necessary limitations briefly and in context.
6. End with a clear, positive next action and Google Play CTA.

## 2. Value-first rule

Lead with what the user CAN do with SIGNA.

Good framing:
- Prepare an image before sharing.
- Use a visible watermark when immediate visual attribution/branding is useful.
- Use an invisible SIGNA signal when you want an additional signal without covering the image.
- Later, use Inspect to check whether a SIGNA signal is observable and review supported image-state information.
- Keep supported processing on the device rather than uploading selected images to a SIGNA server for that processing.

Avoid making these phrases the headline, section title, repeated conclusion, or CTA:
- "not legal proof"
- "not proof of ownership"
- "not proof of originality"
- "not a verdict"
- "only a helper/reference tool"
- equivalent negative-first wording in any locale.

If a boundary is necessary, place it after the benefit and phrase it narrowly. Example: "Resizing, cropping, recompression or screenshots can affect signal observability." Do not turn a necessary caveat into a reason not to use the product.

## 3. Claim-safety boundaries

Never claim or imply:
- legal ownership or legal evidence;
- proof of originality/authenticity;
- 100% protection or impossible removal;
- guaranteed signal survival or detection after arbitrary processing/SNS sharing;
- universal AI-generation detection or certainty that an image is non-AI;
- replacement for encryption, access control, electronic signatures, C2PA, Content Credentials, SynthID, or other provenance systems.

Do not overcorrect by repeating these exclusions. The normal article should communicate product value first; a limitation appears only where the surrounding claim needs it.

## 4. 8-locale native-original rule

Locales:
- en
- ko
- ja
- zh-Hans
- es-419
- pt-BR
- fr
- de

The English article is NOT a translation template.

For every locale:
- write as if the article was conceived by a native editor for that market;
- use locally normal vocabulary, sentence rhythm, examples, messaging habits, and search intent;
- restructure paragraphs and headings when natural;
- use local examples only when genuinely appropriate (e.g. KakaoTalk in Korea, locally normal messaging wording elsewhere);
- avoid calques, English technical sentence order, and identical paragraph-by-paragraph phrasing;
- keep the factual backbone and claim boundaries consistent across locales even when the writing structure differs.

Before calling an 8-locale cluster complete, review every locale independently for translation smell. "Grammatically correct" is not enough.

## 5. Article quality rule

Articles must be useful even before the reader reaches the SIGNA section. Do not write keyword-stuffed SEO pages.

Each article should normally include:
- a scenario-first opening;
- useful explanation/context;
- at least 2 relevant internal Article links;
- a natural transition to SIGNA;
- a concrete SIGNA use flow;
- a Google Play CTA;
- appropriate SEO title/meta;
- Article index and sitemap updates when a new URL is added.

Do not repeat the same disclaimer in the body, callout, SIGNA section and CTA.

## 6. SIGNA product framing

Preferred core framing:
- visible watermark + invisible SIGNA signal;
- prepare before sharing;
- supported local/on-device processing;
- later inspect whether the SIGNA signal is observable and review supported image-state information.

When discussing privacy/local processing, describe what SIGNA actually does. Do not use unsupported fear claims about third-party servers, AI training, leaks, or misuse.

## 7. Existing-content review rule

When editing an existing Article, check whether old claim-safety language has become the dominant message.

Correct when:
- the SIGNA section starts by listing what SIGNA cannot do;
- a CTA ends by telling the reader the product is "not proof" or "only reference information";
- limitations are repeated more often than benefits;
- a section heading is built around "what this does not prove" when a useful positive framing is available;
- the copy leaves a reasonable reader asking "then why would I use SIGNA?"

Preserve factual limitations, but move them to the point where they are actually needed.

## 8. Pre-publish checklist

Do not mark COMPLETE until all are YES:
- Does the article make the user benefit clear?
- Does SIGNA feel useful rather than apologetic?
- Are limitations accurate but secondary?
- Are all product claims production-verified?
- Are all 8 locales native-original rather than translations?
- Did each locale receive an independent language/editorial review?
- Are internal links useful and natural?
- Is the CTA positive and concrete?
- Are SEO/index/sitemap requirements complete?
- Are there no unrelated repository changes?
