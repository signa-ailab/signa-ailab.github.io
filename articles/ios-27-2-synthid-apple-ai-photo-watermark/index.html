iOS 27.2 brings invisible SynthID marks to iPhone AI photos

A photo expanded with AI may look like an ordinary camera shot when it arrives in a chat. Apple’s latest update adds a clue about its creation—but not a verdict on the entire image.

October 28, 2026 · SIGNA AI Lab

From a June announcement to an October release

Apple said in June 2026 that its new generative image tools would carry SynthID. On October 26 it released iOS 27.2. The current iPhone user guide says most images created or edited with Apple Intelligence tools in iOS 27.2 or later include an invisible SynthID watermark. The news is a consumer rollout, not the invention of a new watermarking method.

Consider a camera photograph, a photograph whose edges were extended by a model, and a scene generated entirely in Image Playground. They may arrive as similar-looking files, but they do not share the same production history.

Which edits are marked?

The word “most” matters. Apple does not say every minor adjustment receives the signal. MacRumors tested Extend and Reframe and detected SynthID, while Clean Up results varied with the size of the edit. Those are limited newsroom observations, not an Apple-published threshold. Traditional crops and color adjustments should not be casually equated with generative reconstruction.

Creators who need to explain their process should preserve the camera original, the exported copy and a record of the tools used. A detector result is one clue, not a substitute for that workflow.

Detection is narrower than authenticity

SynthID is embedded in image content rather than stored as an ordinary EXIF text field. Apple points users to SynthID Detector, which looks for compatible signals. A positive result indicates that a supported marker was observed in the file. It does not establish that the depicted event happened, who owns the image, or which individual pixels were generated.

A negative result is equally easy to overread. Not every AI system uses SynthID, not every edit is necessarily marked, and later transformations may affect observability. “No supported signal observed in this copy” is more accurate than “not AI.”

Sharing changes the file being examined

A screenshot creates another image; social services may resize, crop, recompress or alter metadata. Invisible watermarking can be designed to tolerate some transformations, but this rollout does not guarantee survival across every platform and edit chain. Test the exact received file, and retain the source if the distinction matters.

For a technical explanation, see what changes when photos are shared and how invisible watermark detection works.

Different technologies answer different questions

Apple Reference Image addresses a supported camera-capture reference. C2PA / Content Credentials can bind signed provenance assertions to an asset. SynthID is a signal from compatible generative workflows; a visible creator watermark communicates attribution to viewers. Our comparison of these layers explains why none is a universal truth machine.

SIGNA serves a separate, creator-facing moment: on Android, a supported photo can be prepared with a visible mark and invisible SIGNA signal before sharing, then checked later for supported signal and image-state information. It does not detect Apple SynthID or certify that a photo is human-made.
