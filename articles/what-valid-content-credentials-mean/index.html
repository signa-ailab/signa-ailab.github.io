What Does a Valid Content Credential Actually Tell You?

A newsroom receives a photograph with a green verification badge. The badge may be meaningful, but it has not interviewed the photographer, inspected the scene or checked the caption. The first task is to find out precisely which digital statements were checked.

November 10, 2026 · SIGNA AI Lab

The question hidden inside a green badge

Imagine a photo attached to a breaking-news tip. Its credential validates, yet the caption names the wrong city. Both facts can coexist: the signed record can be intact while a description of the scene is mistaken. Treating validation as a verdict about the story makes the technical result do a job it was never designed to do.

C2PA carries assertions about an asset and its processing history. Those assertions may identify a signer, describe actions or connect an ingredient file. Their value depends on what was asserted, by whom, and whether the present file is the one to which the assertion was bound.

Three states, not one truth score

The C2PA 2.5 specification distinguishes Well-Formed, Valid and Trusted manifests. Well-Formed concerns structural and assertion requirements. Valid adds checks such as signature integrity and other validation conditions. Trusted adds a successful trust decision for the signing credential under the validator's configured trust anchors.

A Trusted manifest is therefore also Valid, but the words do not mean that a witness verified the events depicted. The validator reports technical results; the reader still interprets provenance and context. A different trust configuration may also change the trust decision.

A valid manifest and a valid asset are not identical

There is another easily missed distinction. The specification defines an asset as Valid when the portions covered by content bindings have not changed since the active manifest was produced and the active manifest is Valid or Trusted. This is narrower than saying that every possible byte, every earlier version and every real-world claim has been certified.

When a checker says a signature is valid, read the detailed status: was the manifest validated, was its binding to this file checked, and what part of the file was covered? Avoid turning a single badge into an explanation of the entire editing history.

What a failed or missing check can mean

A failed validation can point to a changed file, a malformed record, a signature problem or a trust configuration issue. These are different questions and should be reported separately. A missing credential is even less decisive: the camera may never have produced one, or the particular export may no longer carry it.

A screenshot is a new asset, not a transparent transfer of the source file's metadata. Messaging and publishing workflows can also create new copies. Compare the original export with the received file before attributing an absent credential to deliberate concealment.

A practical five-question reading routine

First, identify the exact file being checked. Second, distinguish manifest status from asset binding. Third, read the signer and the actual assertions instead of only the icon. Fourth, check whether the file is a forwarded copy, screenshot or re-export. Fifth, compare the caption and scene with independent reporting or an earlier source when the claim matters.

For a product photograph, for example, a signed edit history may explain how the background was replaced; it cannot tell you whether the advertised item will arrive. For a press photograph, provenance can help reconstruct workflow without replacing editorial verification.

Where watermarking and SIGNA belong

A visible watermark is a message to people. A compatible invisible signal is something a detector can try to observe in image data. Neither is a C2PA signature. These layers can coexist, but their absence or presence should be reported as separate observations.

SIGNA for Android can prepare supported images with visible watermarks and optional invisible SIGNA signals, and review supported image-state information and SIGNA signals on-device. It is not a cryptographic C2PA signature validator. When checking a received copy, keep the file's technical clues separate from any claim about ownership, AI origin or the truth of a scene.
