Photo privacy · Mobile advertising

What “on-device” tells you about a photo app—and what an ad request reveals

A photo editor can finish rendering an image on your phone and call an ad server in the same session. The renderer reads the photo, applies the changes and writes the result. The advertising component can make a request of its own, independent of the edited file. That distinction matters when a product says it processes photos on-device. The claim describes where the editing happens. To understand what leaves the phone, look at the app’s network activity as well.

November 16, 2026 · SIGNA AI Lab

What an ad server can see on Android

Google says its Mobile Ads Next-Gen SDK for Android collects the device’s IP address by default. Google may use that address to estimate the device’s general location. A server sees the source address of the connection it receives, which can be shared by many devices. Turning off permission for the app to read your location affects one source of location data. Estimating a region from the connection’s IP uses another. Android SDK data disclosure · Google’s location-data explanation · IETF note on IP address sharing

The purpose matters as much as the data field. In its AdMob documentation, Google says its Limited Ads mode still uses IP addresses to get ads to devices. Using an address to deliver a response, keeping it in a log, and combining it with other information for advertising are distinct uses. An ad request on its own cannot tell you whether the address was logged or used for targeting. AdMob’s Limited Ads documentation

What happens when you decline tracking on an iPhone?

Apple’s App Tracking Transparency rules govern tracking as Apple defines it, including linking app data with data from other companies for targeted advertising or ad measurement. When a user declines the request, the app cannot access IDFA, Apple’s advertising identifier. Apple also bars workarounds with other identifiers and prohibits fingerprinting to uniquely identify a device. Google says its iOS SDK can still request an ad following a refusal, with IDFA left out. Its iOS disclosure separately lists coarse location derived from an IP address or obtained through iOS’s approximate-location services where the user permits it. The SDK disclosure describes possible sources of data. It does not establish how a particular request was handled. Apple’s rules for app developers · Requesting ads without IDFA · iOS SDK data disclosure

What changed in 2025 and 2026?

On February 16, 2025, Google changed its own Ads platforms program policies. The UK Information Commissioner’s Office explained that the change removed Google’s own prohibition on fingerprinting for organizations using its ad technology, while applicable UK privacy and consent obligations continued. Separately, Google said it would begin rolling out IP-based advertising measurement and personalization in the EEA, UK and Switzerland from August 2026 using IP addresses it receives through channels including SDKs. IP addresses were already visible to servers before these announcements. Google’s notices explain policy and product changes within its own advertising program. SIGNA, an ad-free Android app from SIGNA AI Lab, the publisher of this article, lets you open a photo already on your phone, add a watermark and save the result offline once the app is installed. You’ll need a connection to start or restore Pro or to download extra fonts for the first time.

What you can check before using a photo app

Try a whole workflow without a connection, starting with a file already on the phone and ending with a saved export. That tells you whether the work depends on a server for the case you tried. On an iPhone, App Privacy Report can then identify domains contacted after you switched the report on. Its network list tells you where connections went. It leaves the contents of individual requests and their purpose unresolved. For the developer’s explanation of what it collects, check Google Play’s Data safety section or the App Store’s App Privacy section. Both systems have exceptions in their public disclosure rules for certain short-lived processing. Apple’s App Privacy Report guide · Google Play Data safety · Apple’s disclosure criteria
